QUESTION:
A person invests Rs. 5,000 at 10% annual compound interest for 2 years. Calculate the final amount.

ANSWER:

EXAMPLE 200

QUESTION:
What is a cash flow statement?

ANSWER:
A cash flow statement reports the cash entering and leaving a business during a period. It classifies cash flows into operating, investing, and financing activities. It helps users understand how a business generates and uses cash.

EXAMPLE 201

QUESTION:
What is gross profit?

ANSWER:
Gross profit is the amount remaining after subtracting the cost of goods sold from revenue. Formula: Gross Profit = Revenue - Cost of Goods Sold. It indicates how much a company earns from its products or services before operating expenses and other costs.

================================================================================
EXAMPLE 202

QUESTION:
A company earns Rs. 5,00,000 in revenue and has a cost of goods sold of Rs. 3,00,000. Calculate gross profit.

EXAMPLE 203

QUESTION:
What is operating profit?

Operating profit is the profit generated from a company's normal business operations after deducting operating expenses from gross profit. It generally excludes interest expenses and income taxes. It helps evaluate the profitability of the company's core operations.

================================================================================
EXAMPLE 204

QUESTION:

ANSWER:

================================================================================
EXAMPLE 205

QUESTION:
What is the difference between revenue and profit?

ANSWER:
Revenue is the total income generated from selling goods or providing services before deducting expenses. Profit is the amount remaining after relevant expenses are deducted from revenue. A company can generate high revenue but still report a low profit or a loss.

================================================================================
EXAMPLE 206

QUESTION:
What is working capital?

ANSWER:
Working capital measures the difference between current assets and current liabilities. Formula: Working Capital = Current Assets - Current Liabilities. It provides an indication of a company's ability to meet short-term financial obligations.

================================================================================
EXAMPLE 207

QUESTION:
A business has current assets of Rs. 4,00,000 and current liabilities of Rs. 2,50,000. Calculate working capital.

EXAMPLE 208

QUESTION:
What is insurance?

Insurance is a financial arrangement in which an insurer provides specified financial protection against covered risks in exchange for a premium. Coverage depends on the policy terms, exclusions, limits, and conditions. Insurance can help reduce the financial impact of unexpected events.

================================================================================
EXAMPLE 209

QUESTION:
What is an insurance premium?

An insurance premium is the amount a policyholder pays to maintain insurance coverage. It may be paid monthly, quarterly, annually, or according to the policy terms. Premium amounts depend on factors such as coverage, risk, age, and the type of insurance.

EXAMPLE 210

QUESTION:
What is a deductible in insurance?

ANSWER:
A deductible is the amount a policyholder must pay toward a covered claim before the insurer pays its eligible share, subject to the policy terms. A higher deductible may reduce the premium in some policies, but it can increase the policyholder's out-of-pocket expense during a claim.

EXAMPLE 211

QUESTION:
What is the difference between life insurance and health insurance?

Life insurance provides a specified financial benefit to beneficiaries or other eligible recipients when the insured person dies, subject to policy conditions. Health insurance helps cover eligible medical expenses according to its terms. The two products address different financial risks.

EXAMPLE 212

QUESTION:
What is term life insurance?

ANSWER:
Term life insurance provides life coverage for a specified period. If the insured person dies during the policy term, the insurer pays the eligible death benefit according to the policy conditions. Traditional term insurance generally focuses on protection rather than investment returns.

EXAMPLE 213

================================================================================
EXAMPLE 214

QUESTION:
What is underinsurance?

EXAMPLE 215

EXAMPLE 218

QUESTION:
What is income tax?

EXAMPLE 219

QUESTION:
What is taxable income?

ANSWER:
Taxable income is the portion of income on which tax is calculated after applying the deductions, exemptions, adjustments, and rules permitted under the relevant tax system. It may differ from a person's total or gross income.

EXAMPLE 220

QUESTION:
What is the difference between gross income and net income?

ANSWER:
Gross income is income before certain deductions and adjustments. Net income is the amount remaining after applicable deductions or expenses, depending on the context. In a salary context, take-home pay is generally the amount received after payroll deductions.

EXAMPLE 221

QUESTION:

A progressive tax system generally applies higher marginal tax rates to higher levels of taxable income. This means that different portions of taxable income may be taxed at different rates. The exact tax brackets and rules depend on the jurisdiction.

EXAMPLE 222

QUESTION:
What is a tax deduction?

ANSWER:
A tax deduction is an amount that eligible taxpayers may subtract from income when calculating taxable income, according to applicable tax rules. Deductions can reduce taxable income, but eligibility, limits, and documentation requirements depend on the tax jurisdiction.

================================================================================
EXAMPLE 223

QUESTION:
What is a tax credit?

ANSWER:
A tax credit is an amount that can reduce the tax payable directly, subject to the applicable rules. Unlike a deduction, which generally reduces taxable income, a tax credit reduces the calculated tax liability. Eligibility and refundability vary by jurisdiction.

================================================================================
EXAMPLE 224

QUESTION:

ANSWER:
Tax planning involves organizing financial activities within the law to understand and manage tax obligations. EXAMPLE 225

QUESTION:
What is the difference between tax avoidance and tax evasion?

Tax avoidance generally refers to arranging financial activities to reduce tax legally using permitted provisions. Tax evasion involves illegally concealing income, falsifying records, or deliberately avoiding tax obligations. Tax evasion can result in penalties and legal consequences.

EXAMPLE 226
